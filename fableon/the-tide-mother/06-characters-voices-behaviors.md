# Characters, Voices and Behaviors — *The Tide Mother*

Each character has a **unique voice**: a different gender, age, pitch and speed, all with an American accent, so viewers know who is speaking even without looking at the screen. Every video prompt also asks for a dry, close, clear voice with **no echo or reverb**. Paste the voice line unchanged into Grok every time they speak, so the voice stays consistent. Each also has **their own physical habits**. The video prompts give each on-screen character one habit per clip, rotating through the list, so no two characters move the same way and nobody repeats the same gesture in every shot.

---

## NARRATOR (voice-over only)
- **Voice prompt:** a deep, warm, unhurried older American male storyteller, calm and resonant, like telling a legend by a fire.
- **Delivery:** long pauses before reveals. He speaks **only in the prologue** (Beats 2–7); after that the film has no narration.

## MAREN — the Tide Mother
- **Look:** about 30, long wet tangled dark-brown hair with thin braids, freckles, grey-green eyes; long black wool dress, a **moss-green knitted shawl** worn over both shoulders all film long and fastened with a carved bone toggle (no black capelet), braided rope belt with an iron net-mending needle, dark leg-wraps and **brown leather sandals**.
- **Voice prompt:** a soft, clear, gentle American voice, warm and smooth in the middle range, calm and tender, with crisp, clean diction (woman, about 30).
- **Signature behaviors:**
  - twists the thin braid on the left when anxious
  - touches the bone net-needle at her belt for comfort
  - lifts her chin when defiant
  - hums three soft notes of an old sea lullaby (this hum returns at the end)
- **Never:** shouts at Tal. She commands him quietly.

## TAL — the dragon son
- **Look (from your three reference sheets):** same designs as your three sheets, recoloured **pale blush-pink with a pearly sheen and violet eyes** (the colour of your reference). Baby: cat-sized, upright, wet translucent pink skin, huge violet eyes. Juvenile: pony-sized, about 4 ft, slender, pink pearly scales, translucent pink wings. Adult: horse-sized, only about 6 ft at the head, head level with Maren's, blush-pink and rose-white scales, pale pink wings with faint violet veins, violet slit-pupil eyes. His fire is yellow-orange.
- **Vocal signature:**
  - baby: crystalline dolphin-like chirps and clicks
  - juvenile: a warbling whale-calf hum
  - adult: a deep whale-song roar with a bell-like ring
- **Signature behaviors:**
  - **tilts his head to the left** when curious, at every age (viewers will recognize him by it)
  - puffs out his cheeks, a callback to "Hold your breath"
  - nudges Maren's hand with his snout, and as an adult lowers his head beside her
  - his glow pulses brighter with emotion

## ODILE — the neighbor
- **Look:** about 45, grey-streaked hair, ruddy cheeks; long faded brick-red wool dress, a thick faded red fringed wool shawl draped over her head as a hood and crossed over her chest, oatmeal linen apron (as in your uploaded sheet).
- **Voice prompt:** a bright, quick, chatty American voice, higher-pitched and cheerful, talking fast with little gasps (woman, about 45).
- **Signature behaviors:**
  - wrings her apron in both hands
  - touches two fingers to her lips and then toward the sea (Kelvhaven's superstitious sign)
  - pulls her fringed red shawl tighter around her head

- **Arc:** her gossip starts the danger, and she's the first to say "We were wrong."

## BRAM — the fish-trader

## HESK — the elder

## Season map and wardrobe (whole film)
The whole film stays in a **chilly early winter**. There is no falling snow, no ice and no frozen sea or harbor; those are saved for a possible season two. Every image and video prompt carries a **Season** line for the weather; the costumes stay the same all film. There is **no narration after the prologue**: each change of season is shown by the landscape, with an optional text card (WEEKS LATER, YEARS LATER). Voices and behaviors never change. All costumes stay **medieval** (northern fishing village, around 1100 AD): hand-woven wool, linen, fleece, fur and leather, with no zippers, modern buttons, printed patterns or modern footwear.

## Cinematic rules (applied to every image and video prompt)

**0. 6-second beats, one image each.** Every beat is **exactly 6 seconds** (Grok's clip length) and starts from **its own image**. Generate each one as a 6-second Grok video; no trimming. Never chain a clip from the last frame of the previous clip, because that is where faces, costumes and voices drift. Each clip has at most one speaking character, and their voice line is repeated in every clip. Narration (prologue only) is recorded separately and laid over the clips in the edit.

**1. Left and right, facing each other, faces always visible.** Whenever two people or two groups face each other (person to person, person to group, group to person, group to group), one side is on the **left** of the frame and the other on the **right**, both turned toward each other in three-quarter view. Nobody ever has their back to the camera. **This applies only to interaction or confrontation.** When people move together in the same direction toward a goal (the procession leading Maren to the cliff, following, walking side by side, being led, shoved from behind), they are **not** split left against right: they are one group moving one way, shot from the side in three-quarter view (Beats 032, 122, 222–224, 230, 235, 274).  Characters who talk **face each other**, with their bodies turned about 45° toward the camera so every face is clearly visible (three-quarter view). Nobody ever has their back to the camera. One side of the conversation always stays on the **left** of the frame and the other on the **right**, so their eyelines meet. A speaker looks at **whoever they are talking to**. If the line names someone else ("Tal! No!"), they turn to look at that person, and if they refer to an object, they look at it, then back. Each speaking beat is a three-quarter **close-up or medium close-up** of the speaker, looking toward the listener just out of frame; silent beats are **two-shots** or **reaction close-ups** of the listener.

**2. The camera DNA of the reference film** (*She Gave Birth to a Dragon Part 2*, shots 1:00–3:15 and 7:00–11:00 analysed). That film mixes shot sizes in roughly equal parts and lets them carry the drama. This pack follows the same mix:
- **Wide shots** (about a third), often from a **low angle**, open every scene and show the place, the crowd and Tal's size. About 1 in 10 beats is a **slow pan** across a wide shot, following someone walking, running, flying or swimming, or revealing the landscape.
- **Medium shots and medium two-shots** (about a third) carry dialogue and interaction: characters face each other, three-quarter to the camera, at eye level.
- **Close-ups** (about a third) carry emotion, with an occasional **extreme close-up on the eyes** at the strongest moments, and a **slight low angle** for menace (Bram, Hesk, the mob).
- **Within each scene:** an establishing wide → medium for the interaction → close-ups for emotion and reactions.
- **Every other beat is static**, locked off on a tripod, with no zooms.

**2b. Natural posture and memorable close-ups.**
- **Quiet, emotional moments** (in the cave, the cottage or on the beach, when the mood is tender, nostalgic, grieving or loving): Maren **sits** on the cave ledge, a low rock or the edge of her bed, and Tal **lies down beside her** with his legs folded under him and his head raised, listening, like a calm animal resting. Baby Tal rests in her lap or the basket. Neither stands up during these beats.
- **When Maren tells Tal a story or opens her heart to him** (for example Beats 103–111, about Tal's father): **close two-shots** of the two of them, Maren sitting, Tal lying beside her, both faces visible. Grok cannot keep Tal consistent if he drops out of the frame between beats, so he stays in shot.
- **Face-only close-ups are used only when Maren is truly alone**, for example in her cottage thinking or talking to herself (Beats 041–043) or in labour in the cave (Beat 013), and for silent thoughtful moments such as the cliff edge (Beat 232).

**2c. Grok Master Prompt format (every image prompt).** Each image prompt has five parts, in this order:
- **[ASPECT RATIO]** 16:9 cinematic widescreen.
- **[FRAMING & LENS]** the shot size and angle (**medium wide shot** for full-length shots), a **35mm anamorphic lens** and **cinematic framing**, with plenty of space so the subjects never touch or get cut off by the top and bottom edges.
- **[LIGHTING]** **cinematic lighting**: the scene's light plus a dramatic style for the time of day:
  - golden-hour light at dawn and dusk;
  - Rembrandt lighting and volumetric haze from a single flame at night and indoors;
  - volumetric firelight for torches and dragon fire;
  - volumetric light through sea mist on grey days;
  - volumetric light shafts underwater.
- **[SUBJECT & ACTION]** the characters, what they do, who faces whom, the state of every object, and the setting.
- **[AESTHETIC STYLE]** **photorealistic**, **shallow depth of field** with cinematic bokeh, dark-fantasy film still, fine grain, desaturated high-contrast grade, static camera, professional movie look.

Always set **16:9 in Grok's aspect-ratio setting** before generating; the prompt also asks for 16:9 widescreen in words.


**3. One speaker at a time.** Each line has its own time window that names the **SPEAKER** and how to recognise them ("the bearded man in the black coat"). Everyone else is **SILENT, mouths closed**, and there is a short silence between lines.

**4. Charges and processions are shot from the side, never from behind.** In the charge at the cave (Beats 184–186), the mob is on the **left** running right, and Maren and Tal are on the **right** facing them, with faces visible on both sides. The gap closes from 70 to 35 to 10 meters across the frame. The procession (Beats 222–224) is **one group walking together in one direction**: Maren and Hesk at its head, the villagers close behind, all heading for the drowning stone. Nobody faces anyone else, so there is no left-versus-right split.

**5. Real distance.** Each face-off keeps a fixed distance: about 2 m indoors, 3–4 m for two people outdoors, 6 m between a crowd and Maren, and 20 m between the jetty and Tal in the harbor.

**6. Crowds behave like real people, depending on the scene.**
- **Everyday scenes** (market, lanes, harbor by day): busy village life. People haggle, hand over coins, gut and salt fish, carry baskets and barrels, mend nets and chat, and children run between the stalls. Everyone faces a different direction and gets on with their own task; nobody stands staring.
- **Gatherings by day** (the wreck at dawn, the judgment in the square, the cliff top): people stand in loose, uneven groups, pointing, whispering and holding their children close.
- **The mob at night:** the villagers hold their ground and wait for Bram's command, each making small movements of their own, with torches at uneven heights.
- **Public places are never empty:** in every daytime beat at the market, in the lanes or at the harbor, even a close conversation between two characters has villagers going about their business in the background, slightly out of focus. They never stop to stare.
- **Lights by time of day:** lanterns, torches and candles are carried **only at night or indoors in the dark**, never in daylight.

**7. Nobody looks into the lens.**

**8. True night.** At night the sky is black, faces are lit only by the nearby flame or Tal's glow, and everything else falls into black. There is no daylight look.

**9. Four fixed medieval light sources.** Each looks the same in every scene:
| Light | Design |
|---|---|
| **Clay oil lamp** | A shallow fired-clay bowl of fish oil with a single wick, used indoors |
| **Tallow candle** | Thick and yellowish, stuck in its own drippings, used indoors and in the cave |
| **Horn lantern** | A small black iron-framed lantern with scraped-horn panes and a tallow candle inside, the only lantern in the film |
| **Pitch torch** | A wooden stave with a rag head soaked in fish oil and pitch, carried by the mob |
