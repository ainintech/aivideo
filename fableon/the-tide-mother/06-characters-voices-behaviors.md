# Characters, Voices and Behaviors — *The Tide Mother*

Each character has a **unique voice** (paste the voice line unchanged into Grok every time they speak, so the voice stays consistent) and **their own physical habits**. The video prompts give each on-screen character one habit per clip, rotating through the list, so no two characters move the same way and nobody repeats the same gesture in every shot.

## NARRATOR (voice-over only)
- **Voice prompt:** a deep, resonant, unhurried older male storyteller with warm gravel and a faint Nordic lilt, like telling a legend by a fire.
- **Delivery:** long pauses before reveals. He speaks **only in the prologue** (Beats 2–7); after that the film has no narration.

## MAREN — the Tide Mother
- **Look:** about 30, long wet tangled dark-brown hair with thin braids, freckles, grey-green eyes; long black wool dress, a **moss-green knitted shawl** worn over both shoulders all film long and fastened with a carved bone toggle (no black capelet), braided rope belt with an iron net-mending needle, dark leg-wraps and **brown leather sandals**.
- **Voice prompt:** soft husky alto with a gentle northern-coastal lilt, breathy when afraid, sentences trailing off quietly.
- **Signature behaviors:**
  - twists the thin braid on the left when anxious
  - touches the bone net-needle at her belt for comfort
  - lifts her chin when defiant
  - hums three soft notes of an old sea lullaby (this hum returns at the end)
- **Never:** shouts at Tal. She commands him quietly.

## TAL — the dragon son
- **Look (from your three reference sheets):** same designs as your three sheets, recoloured **pale blush-pink with a pearly sheen and violet eyes** (the colour of your reference). Baby: cat-sized, upright, wet translucent pink skin, huge violet eyes. Juvenile: pony-sized, about 4 ft, slender, pink pearly scales, translucent pink wings. Adult: horse-sized, only about 6 ft at the head, head level with Maren's, blush-pink and rose-white scales, pale pink wings with faint violet veins, violet slit-pupil eyes. His fire is yellow-orange.
- **Vocal signature:**
  - baby: crystalline dolphin-like chirps and clicks
  - juvenile: a warbling whale-calf hum
  - adult: a deep whale-song roar with a bell-like ring
- **Signature behaviors:**
  - **tilts his head to the left** when curious, at every age (viewers will recognize him by it)
  - puffs out his cheeks, a callback to "Hold your breath"
  - nudges Maren's hand with his snout, and as an adult lowers his head beside her
  - his glow pulses brighter with emotion

## ODILE — the neighbor

## Season map and wardrobe (whole film)
The whole film stays in a **chilly early winter**. There is no falling snow, no ice and no frozen sea or harbor; those are saved for a possible season two. Every image and video prompt carries a **Season** line for the weather; the costumes stay the same all film. There is **no narration after the prologue**: each change of season is shown by the landscape, with an optional text card (WEEKS LATER, YEARS LATER). Voices and behaviors never change. All costumes stay **medieval** (northern fishing village, around 1100 AD): hand-woven wool, linen, fleece, fur and leather, with no zippers, modern buttons, printed patterns or modern footwear.

## Cinematic rules (applied to every image and video prompt)

**0. Short clips, one image each.** Every clip is **3–6 seconds** and starts from **its own image** (Clip 099-1, 099-2…). Generate each one as a 6-second Grok video and trim it. Never chain a clip from the last frame of the previous clip, because that is where faces, costumes and voices drift. Each clip has at most one speaking character, and their voice line is repeated in every clip. Narration (prologue only) is recorded separately and laid over the clips in the edit.

**1. Shoot from behind the listener.** In every conversation or face-off, the person listening is in the foreground with their **back to the camera**, and the speaker is in the background **facing the camera**. One side shows faces and the other shows backs, so the two sides always face each other. When both sides speak, the beat has an **A/B pair of shots**, each with its own image (for example Shot 153A from behind Tal and Maren to see Bram, and Shot 153B from behind Bram to see Tal and Maren). Put the clips back to back in the edit.

**2. Aerial view first, then close-ups.** Every conversation clip opens with a high aerial view of everyone, so viewers see where all the characters stand. Then the camera moves in to a close-up of each speaker, using a slow push-in, a dolly-in, a crash zoom or an extreme close-up. The camera always stays on the same side of the action (the 180° rule).

**3. One speaker at a time.** Each line has its own time window that names the **SPEAKER** and how to recognise them ("the bearded man in the black coat"). Everyone else is **SILENT, mouths closed**, and there is a short silence between lines.

**4. Runs, charges and processions are shot from behind.** The camera follows the runners from behind and above, so we only see their backs. What they run toward faces the camera, far away and small. The gap closes gradually over the whole clip: about 70 m to 35 m to 10 m in 15 seconds.

**5. Real distance.** Each face-off keeps a fixed distance: about 2 m indoors, 3–4 m for two people outdoors, 6 m between a crowd and Maren, and 20 m between the jetty and Tal in the harbor.

**6. The crowd holds its position.** Villagers wait for their leader. Each one makes small movements of their own at different moments (glancing down, looking up, turning to look behind, shifting their weight, whispering), with their lights held at uneven heights. They never move in step as one block.

**7. Nobody looks into the lens.**

**8. True night.** At night the sky is black, faces are lit only by the nearby flame or Tal's glow, and everything else falls into black. There is no daylight look.

**9. Four fixed medieval light sources.** Each looks the same in every scene:
| Light | Design |
|---|---|
| **Clay oil lamp** | A shallow fired-clay bowl of fish oil with a single wick, used indoors |
| **Tallow candle** | Thick and yellowish, stuck in its own drippings, used indoors and in the cave |
| **Horn lantern** | A small black iron-framed lantern with scraped-horn panes and a tallow candle inside, the only lantern in the film |
| **Pitch torch** | A wooden stave with a rag head soaked in fish oil and pitch, carried by the mob |
