Companion to production-bible.md and generation-prompts.md. This file rebuilds the whole film as 6-second Grok Imagine clips and spells out the exact camera work, lenses, light and color for every shot. The story stays the same; this file is about the picture.

Runtime: 3:00 | Clips: 30 (all 6s) | Aspect: 16:9 | 24fps look


1. Why it "glows" (the look in one list)

The look comes from these things, not from the story. Put them in every shot.

* Backlight. The sun, a window or a lamp sits behind or beside the actors, so hair and shoulders get a bright golden rim. Never light faces flat from the front.
* Diffusion filter. A 1/8 Black Pro-Mist filter makes highlights bloom softly and gives a gentle halo around light sources. This is the "glow".
* Shallow depth of field. Close shots use f/1.4–f/2. The face is sharp; the background melts into soft round bokeh.
* Long lenses on faces. Close-ups use 85mm–100mm. Faces look flattering and the background compresses and blurs.
* Slow camera. Every move is slow and smooth (dolly, slider, gimbal, drone). Nothing fast, nothing shaky except a light breathing handheld on emotional beats.
* Warm film color. Kodak Portra 400 look: warm highlights, lifted (never black) shadows, soft contrast, fine grain.
* Light moving across faces. Tree shadows, curtain light, a door opening — light that changes during the shot keeps the eye glued to the screen.

Glow tag — paste at the end of every Grok prompt:
shot on ARRI Alexa Mini LF, 1/8 Black Pro-Mist filter, soft halation and highlight bloom, warm golden backlight with rim light on hair, creamy shallow depth of field, Kodak Portra 400 film look, lifted warm shadows, soft contrast, fine film grain, 24fps cinematic motion, photorealistic

Negative / avoid line — paste after the glow tag:
no text, no subtitles, no watermark, no fast camera, no shaky camera, no cold blue light, no harsh flash, no plastic skin, no cartoon, no distorted hands

4. Lens and Camera Cheat Sheet (what each choice does to the viewer)

Focal lengths:

* 16–24mm (wide) — big space, establishing, aerials. Makes rooms feel open.
* 32–35mm (normal-wide) — walking and following shots. Feels like you are there.
* 50mm (normal) — two-shots and medium shots. Honest, human view.
* 85mm (portrait) — close-ups. Most flattering face, background turns to soft bokeh.
* 100mm macro — extreme close-ups of hands, eyes, details.
* 135mm (long) — spying from far away, strong background blur and compression.

Shot sizes:

* EWS extreme wide / aerial — place and scale.
* WS wide — whole body plus room.
* MS medium — waist up.
* MCU medium close-up — chest up.
* CU close-up — face fills frame.
* ECU extreme close-up — eyes, hands, a detail.

Camera angles:

* Eye level — neutral, real.
* Low angle — camera below eyes looking up; feels big, hopeful.
* High angle — camera above looking down; feels gentle, small, tender.
* Top-down / bird's-eye — camera straight down at 90 degrees; graphic and beautiful.
* Over-the-shoulder (OTS) — one person's shoulder soft in the foreground.
* Profile — side of the face, great with window light.

Camera movements:

* Push-in (dolly in) — move slowly toward the face; adds emotion.
* Pull-back (dolly out) — move away; reveal or ending.
* Tracking / follow — move with the actor.
* Leading — camera walks backward in front of the actor.
* Arc / orbit — circle around the subject.
* Crane / boom up — camera rises.
* Slider — slow sideways slide.
* Rack focus — focus moves from one subject to another.
* Drone glide — slow aerial flight forward, descend, or top-down track.


5. Shot List (30 clips, all 6 seconds)

Every clip is 6 seconds. Short clips keep faces, clothes and rooms consistent — Grok drifts less in 6 seconds than in 10 or 15.

The rhythm does not come from clip length any more. It comes from changing the camera: every new clip changes the lens, the angle or the movement, so the film never feels repetitive. A long moment (like the hug) is covered by several 6s clips from different angles, the way real films cut a big scene.

Each clip lists: story beat, shot size + angle, lens + aperture, movement, light, palette, then the full Grok prompt to copy. One action and one camera move per clip.

7. How to Generate in Grok

1. Make the start frame first. Use Grok Imagine (image) with the same prompt, minus the movement words. Pick the best image.
2. Animate that image at 6 seconds with the full prompt.
3. Write the camera first. Grok follows the beginning of the prompt most strongly, so shot size, angle and lens come early.
4. One action and one camera move per clip. If a clip tries to do two things, split it into two clips.
5. Never change the character blocks. Same words, same clothes, every time.
6. Chain clips in the same scene. When the next clip continues the same action from a similar angle (for example 4 → 5, 24 → 25, 27 → 29), save the last frame of the clip you just made and use it as the start frame of the next one. This is the strongest trick for consistency.
7. Same room, same start image. For the bedroom (14–20), sitting room (10–11, 22–29) and hallway (12, 21), reuse the same room picture as the base so the space does not change.
8. If Grok makes the move too fast, add "very slow, smooth, gentle" in front of the move.
9. If a face drifts, regenerate from the start frame. Do not accept a different face.
10. Song: the birthday song is split across clips 27–29. In editing, lay one continuous recording of the song over all three clips so it does not jump between clips.
11. Grade all 30 clips the same way at the end (production-bible.md section 7), then add music: soft piano starting at clip 13, rising through clips 24–30.
