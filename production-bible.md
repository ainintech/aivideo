The Surprise — Production Bible

Runtime: ~80s | Clips: 13 | Locations: 6

1. Characters

* Daniel — husband, 34. Short dark brown hair, light stubble, warm brown eyes, olive-tan skin, average-tall build. Navy blue button-up (sleeves rolled), dark jeans, brown leather watch. Plans and delivers the surprise.
* Sarah — wife, 32. Wavy chestnut hair, hazel eyes, fair-medium skin, petite. Cream silk pajamas, later a blush-pink cardigan. The birthday woman — sleeping, woken, then surprised.
* Emily — friend, 33, unseen for 10+ years. Straight black hair in a low ponytail, tall, slim, caramel skin, bright smile. Cream oversized sweater, denim jacket, beige trousers, carry-on suitcase. The surprise herself.

2. Locations

* Airport arrivals curb — ext., overcast daylight, glass facade, luggage carts.
* Car interior — daylight through windows, blurred streets passing.
* House driveway — ext., late golden-hour light.
* Sitting room — int., sofa, floor lamp, family photos, sheer curtains.
* Hallway — int., connects sitting room to bedroom, warm wood floor.
* Bedroom — int., linen curtains, golden light, neutral bedding.

5. Generation Order

Characters → Locations → Still scenes (per clip) → Grade on all stills → Video (clip-by-clip, assembled 1–13).
Never generate a still before its characters + location are locked; never generate video before the still is graded.

6. Aesthetic Direction

A warm, intimate home-movie-turned-cinematic memory. Shallow depth of field, natural motivated light — window light, golden hour outside, warm practical lamps inside. Gentle handheld camera, present but unobtrusive. Colors warm and soft, no harsh shadows or cold fluorescent light. Locations lived-in, not staged. Expressions naturalistic and understated until the reveal, where warmth is allowed to peak. Must stay identical across characters, locations, stills, and video.

7. Color Grading

Yes — one grade applied to all 13 stills before video. Warm cinematic tone, soft golden highlights, lifted shadows (never crushed), low-medium contrast, warm white balance (~3800–4500K) even on exteriors, protected warm skin tones, muted pastel saturation, subtle vignette, light film grain. Lock the same settings across every still so nothing drifts.

Reusable grade tag:
Warm cinematic tone, golden hour and warm lamp light, soft Kodak Portra 400 film emulation, low-medium contrast, lifted shadows, muted pastel saturation, protected warm skin tones, subtle vignette, fine grain, a24-style domestic drama
